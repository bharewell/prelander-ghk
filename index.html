I used to avoid certain angles in photos. Not because I was vain — because the crow’s feet had started announcing themselves under every overhead light, and the under-eye bags made me look like I hadn’t slept in a week even when I had.

At 49, the sagging along my jawline felt sudden. The fine lines around my mouth. The way foundation settled into creases it never used to find. My face had started working against the woman I still felt like on the inside.

My doctor said it was normal for my age. I hated that answer. Not emotionally — intellectually. I know the biology is changing. I wanted to understand the mechanism, not be told to accept it.

What I was seeing wasn’t “just getting older.” Estrogen helps drive collagen synthesis signaling in the skin. As that signal declines in perimenopause, the texture, elasticity, and firmness shift — not dramatically at first, but enough that you notice under certain light, in certain photos, in the mirror at 7am. Crow’s feet. Under-eye bags. A jawline that used to hold.

It was specific. It was biological. And it was not something a better eye cream was going to reverse.

I had already done the “right” things. Retinol. Hyaluronic acid. Better SPF. Sleep. Collagen powders. Fancy eye creams that cost more than a nice dinner. Some of it helped around the edges. None of it closed the gap.

Friends started talking about Botox the way people talk about getting their teeth cleaned — routine, expected, inevitable. I wasn’t against it. I was against the idea that the only path forward was freezing parts of my face every three months for the rest of my life.

HRT helps many women. I respect that. But hormonal replacement addresses the signal. It does not directly research what happens downstream in the tissue — the collagen pathways, the repair signaling, the skin’s ability to hold structure. That gap is what sent me looking further.
